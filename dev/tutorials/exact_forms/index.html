Exact values

A recoupling value already has a short exact description, its formula. Exact() and Exact(k) write that formula in quantum integers [n], and never expand it.

using QRecoupling, Nemo
c = q6j(Exact(), 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1)
([5] − 1)/[4]!   (1/6 at q = 1)

Every q, and q = 1

Exact() needs no value of q, and holds at all of them. Evaluate it with c(q) at a real positive q. Used as a number, it means its value at q = 1, and classical gives that value exactly.

(c == 1//6, classical(c), Float64(c), c(0.8))
(true, 1//6, 0.16666666666666666, 0.17048258800366134)

classical returns a sign and a rational under a root, with its own arithmetic.

r = q3j(Exact(), 1//2, 1//2, 0, 1//2, -1//2, 0)
(r, classical(r), classical(r)^2)
(q^(1/2) √(1/[2]), √(1//2), 1//2)

Between two Exact() values, == means equal at every q. The integer 6 is [6] and [3]! at q = 1, but those are different functions of q.

(qint(Exact(), 6), qfact(Exact(), 3), qint(Exact(), 6) == qfact(Exact(), 3))
([6], [3]!, false)

Nothing is expanded to decide this. Clearing denominators gives an integer polynomial in q, and a nonzero one cannot vanish at an integer larger than the sum of its coefficients. So the test reads that polynomial at one such integer, through its residues at word primes, and stops at the first nonzero residue. A vanishing sum is shown as 0. A value can vanish at q = 1 without vanishing elsewhere, and then its form is not 0 while v == 0 is true.

A level

Exact(k) is the same value at q = e^{iπ/(k+2)}. The labels fold to n ≤ (k+2)/2, the relations among the q-integers simplify the form, and zeros are found and proved.

v = q6j(Exact(10), 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1)
([5] − 1)/[4]!   (k = 10)   ≈ 0.1547005
(q6j(Exact(3), 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1), q6j(Exact(10), 1, 3//2, 3//2, 2, 3//2, 3//2))
(−1/[2]²   (k = 3), 0   (k = 10))

Two equal values can print differently, so == decides equality and the text does not. Nothing is expanded to do it. == compares the squares of the two values at places, primes where q is a root of unity, reading each from the rule's factorials. One different residue settles it, in about 5 µs at spins 100, k = 401. Equal squares need more places, as many as the norm of the difference allows, about 6.5 ms at that size, and the certified number then gives the sign.

Arithmetic with *, +, -, / and ^ is exact at a level, needs no backend, and gives a value that prints in the basis [1], …, [d].

a = q6j(Exact(12), 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1)
(a^2, a * inv(a) == 1)
(LevelValue(k = 12, 10[5] − 33[3] + 52), true)

A form holds its rule, which is all that ==, iszero and float read, so it costs about 0.15 µs at any size. What it prints is written the first time it is shown, about 490 µs at spins 100, k = 401.

Use Level(k) for numbers. It costs about 1 µs at that size, and is accurate to about 10⁻¹⁶ in the median. numeric_value(v; bits) gives more digits.

(Float64(v), numeric_value(v; bits = 128))
(0.15470053837925152, 0.1547005383792515290182975610039149112949)

Powers of q

qcg and q3j carry powers of q, printed in front and on each term. At a level they reduce by q^h = −1, and the value is complex.

qcg(Exact(9), 3//2, 1//2, 1, 0, 3//2, 1//2)
q²(q⁶ + [2]) √(1/([3][5]))   (k = 9, q = e^{iπ/11})   ≈ 0.3125442 + 0.5841254im

== and iszero decide these forms like any other. They have no arithmetic at a level.

Radicals

With Nemo loaded, radical rewrites a level value in nested square roots.

radical(v)
(2√3 − 3)/3
no_surd = radical(q6j(Exact(5), 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1))
no_surd
no radical form: v² has degree 3 over ℚ at level 5, and only a power of two is a tower of square roots
  (`v.x_value` gives (P, R), which is then the value's only closed form)
  ≈ 0.08814600002041936

A radical exists only when the square of the value has degree a power of two over ℚ, which is rare beyond small levels. radical returns a RadExpr when it succeeds, otherwise a NoRadical with a reason:

kindMeaning
:noneNo expression in real nested square roots exists
:untriedThe degree budget prevented the search
:longThe expression exceeds an explicitly requested length budget
:failedThe descent could not finish its required sign checks

Here “radical” means nested square roots, not arbitrary nth roots. The default degree_limit is 32; maxlen=0 imposes no expression-length cap. Increasing the degree limit can be very expensive in both time and memory.

Polynomials in x

With Nemo loaded, QRecoupling.xpolynomial(v) and QRecoupling.radicand(v) give a level value as P(x)√R(x) with x = 2cos(π/(k+2)), and x_form expands a generic expression in x = q + q⁻¹. Both carry out the sum, which grows quickly with the spins and is not needed to read a value or to decide one.

s = q6j(Symbolic(), 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1)
g = x_form(s)       # (x² − 3)/(x⁴ − 3x² + 2)
Exact expression in x = q + q⁻¹
  = (x² − 3) / (x⁴ − 3x² + 2)

Symbolic() retains a factorial rule, without evaluating the sum during construction or display. Radicals are displayed as products of ψ factors, where ψe(x) is the minimal polynomial of 2cos(2π/e). `phiform(value)requests cyclotomic factorization explicitly. Braiding and twist phases preserve their branch throughu² − x·u + 1 = 0, withu = q`: x alone cannot distinguish q from its reciprocal.

Generic square roots specify an algebraic expression. To evaluate with the package's numerical branch convention, use qeval(s; q=...) or a level target on the original symbolic value.

API reference

QRecoupling.QForm — Type
QForm

A value written in q-integers, as returned by Exact(), Exact(k) and qform.

A generic form holds at every q. f(q) evaluates it at a real positive q, and used as a number it means its value at q = 1: classical gives that exactly, and == with a number, float, numeric_value and arithmetic read it. Between two forms == means equal at every q, decided exactly.

A form at a level is one number, complex when it carries powers of q. float and numeric_value give it, and ==, iszero and arithmetic are exact.

A form holds its rule, which is short and is all that deciding and evaluating read. What it prints is written from the rule the first time it is shown, so a form costs no more than its rule until then.

source
QRecoupling.classical — Function
classical(f::QForm) -> ClassicalResult

The value of a generic form at q = 1, exactly: a sign and the rational under the root. It compares with rationals and integers, and converts with Rational when it is one.

classical(q6j(Exact(), 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1))          # 1//6
classical(q3j(Exact(), 2, 2, 0, 1, -1, 0))         # -√(1//5)

QRecoupling.SymbolicValue — Type
SymbolicValue

A recoupling symbol as an exact expression in q, with no level and no evaluation: what Symbolic() returns. It retains the factorial rule without evaluating the sum.

Displayed as the rule, a finite factorial sum in x = q + q⁻¹, whatever the labels are. Printing carries out no sum or polynomial factorization. Its output length is bounded; the cost of formatting factorial and ψ factors still depends on the rule.

Expanding is a separate request, and which of the two you want depends on the basis:

cost at j = 6
x_form(v)the sum carried out in x: √(∏ψ)·P(x)/Q(x)0.3 ms
phi_form(v)the sum carried out in q and factored over the cyclotomics10 ms

phi_form is thirty to fifty times the cost of x_form on the same symbol, and essentially all of the difference is one call to factor over ℤ[q]; see its docstring. x_form is cached on the value, phi_form is not.

Exact(k) specialises the same expression to a level.

source
QRecoupling.x_form — Function
x_form(v::SymbolicValue) -> XValue

The exact value for generic q as √(∏_{e ∈ rad} ψ_e(x)) · num(x)/den(x), x = q + q⁻¹.

Named for its pair: x_form(v) carries the sum out in x, phi_form carries it out in q and factors it, which costs 30–50× as much (0.26 ms against 10.1 ms on {6 6 6; 6 6 6}). Use x_form unless the cyclotomic factors are what you are after.

It was called xvalue, one letter from ExactX's v.x_value and meaning something else — that one is the stored (P, R) of a value at a level, this one is an expansion of a generic-q rule. The old name still works and warns; it goes in later version.

Expansion is explicit and may still be expensive at large labels; nothing about displaying v triggers it. The result is cached on the symbolic value, so asking twice is free; callers receive an owned copy, so mutating its polynomial coefficients cannot corrupt later requests or display. Square roots describe the formal algebraic expression; numerical evaluation uses the rule's branch convention.

QRecoupling.radical — Function
radical(v::ExactX; maxlen = 0, degree_limit = 32) -> RadExpr or NoRadical

The value in real nested square roots, e.g. (√5 − 3)/2, or a NoRadical saying why there is none. No length limit by default (maxlen > 0 adds one); the descent is exponential in the value's degree, so past degree_limit it is not attempted and the number is given instead.

radical(q6j(Exact(3), 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1))    # (√5 − 3)/2, the Fibonacci level
radical(q6j(Exact(5), 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1))    # no radical form: v² has degree 3 over ℚ at level 5, …
radical(q6j(Exact(254), 45, 45, 45, 30, 30, 30))          # degree 64: not attempted, with the number
radical(q6j(Exact(254), 45, 45, 45, 30, 30, 30); degree_limit = 64)   # …and this is the long wait
